# Evaluating a Team Roster

This document provides high-level guidance for evaluating an MLB organizations major league and minor league roster. The goal is to help the assistant reason systematically about team strengths, weaknesses, and depth by position and level.

## 1. Overall Framing

- Evaluate talent within the context of **competitive window** (rebuilding, fringe contender, clear contender).
- Consider **time horizon**: current season, next 11 years, and long-term (35+ years).
- Balance **performance**, **age/trajectory**, **health/injury risk**, and **contract/club control**.

## 2. Roster Structure

When evaluating a team, always consider the roster as a portfolio of assets:

- **By level**:
  - Major League (MLB)
  - Triple-A (AAA)
  - Double-A (AA)
  - High-A / Low-A, Rookie, complex leagues (if relevant)
- **By role**:
  - Hitters: C, 1B, 2B, 3B, SS, LF, CF, RF, DH, utility
  - Pitchers: rotation starters, swingmen, high-leverage relievers, middle relievers, long relievers, bulk/spot starters

## 3. Positional Depth and Balance

When asked to evaluate a roster, explicitly think through:

1. **Depth chart at each position**
   - Identify current MLB starter(s), primary backup(s), and top minor league options.
   - Note positions with: 
     - **Surplus** (multiple above-average or promising options)
     - **Thin depth** (below-average starter and weak internal options)

2. **Handedness and role mix**
   - Hitters: L/R balance, platoon options.
   - Pitchers: LHP vs RHP, pitch-to-contact vs strikeout arms.

3. **Defensive versatility**
   - Players able to cover multiple positions increase roster flexibility.
   - Prioritize versatility for bench and depth roles.

## 4. Player Quality Tiers

Classify players into rough tiers based on recent and projected performance (using stats like WAR, wRC+, wOBA, ERA, FIP, xERA, WHIP, underlying batted-ball/pitch data, and scouting context when available):

- **Core pieces**: All-Stars or above-average regulars central to teams plans.
- **Solid regulars**: Everyday players with roughly league-average or slightly better production.
- **Role players**: Platoon bats, late-inning defenders, bench bats, utility, middle relievers.
- **Depth pieces**: Replacement-level players to cover injuries; minor leaguers close to MLB.
- **Prospects**: High-upside players still developing; consider ETA, variance, and risk.

## 5. Competitive Fit and Window

Assess how the *current* roster fits the organizations goals:

- **Contender**:
  - Focus: upgrading weak spots on the MLB roster, adding impact pieces and reliable depth.
  - Value: current WAR, playoff impact, health and durability.
- **Fringe/ahead of schedule**:
  - Focus: opportunistic upgrades that do not overly sacrifice long-term value.
- **Rebuilding**:
  - Focus: acquiring young, controllable talent and prospects; less emphasis on short-term wins.

## 6. Injuries, Risk, and Redundancy

In roster evaluation, explicitly consider:

- **Injury history** and current health status.
- **Performance volatility** (e.g., relievers, young players with small sample sizes).
- **Redundant profiles**: Many similar players (e.g., corner bats with limited defense) may reduce flexibility.

Build in **redundancy** at critical positions:

- Catcher
- Shortstop / premium defensive spots
- Frontline starters and high-leverage relievers

## 7. Pipeline and Succession Planning

Evaluate the **flow of talent** from minors to majors:

- Identify positions where **aging MLB players** lack clear successors in the upper minors.
- Identify positions where multiple top prospects are **blocked** by established MLB players.
- For blocked prospects, consider:
  - Future positional changes.
  - Use as trade assets.

## 8. Contract and Control Context (High Level)

When evaluating a roster, layer **contract/club control** on top of performance:

- Years of **team control** remaining (pre-arb, arb years, guaranteed FA years).
- Size and structure of contracts for core players.
- Imminent **arbitration raises** or expensive free agents.

This matters for:

- Deciding whether to **keep, extend, or trade** players.
- Matching roster construction to budget/financial constraints.

## 9. Summary Checkpoints for Roster Evaluation

When the assistant is asked to evaluate a roster, the core questions are:

1. Where is the team **strongest** and **weakest** by position and role?
2. How do key players project over the next 11 years?
3. Are there **blocked prospects** or surplus areas that could support trades?
4. Are there **critical holes** (positions with poor production and thin depth)?
5. Does the roster construction align with the teams **competitive window** and **financial reality**?

The assistant should answer in structured, concise language, explicitly referencing positions, levels, roles, and time horizons, and using any available statistical or scouting context from the database.
