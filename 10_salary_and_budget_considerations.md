# Salary, Budget, and Contract Considerations (High Level)

This document provides high-level guidance on how the assistant should factor **salary, budget, and contracts** into roster decisions, trades, and long-term planning. It is not a full CBA reference; instead, it gives conceptual rules for realistic reasoning.

## 1. Salary and Team Budget Context

Teams operate under different **budget constraints**:

- Large-market teams can generally carry higher payrolls and may be willing to take on expensive contracts for short-term gains.
- Small- or mid-market teams often prioritize **cost-controlled players** and must manage payroll more carefully.

The assistant should:

- Assume each team has a **finite budget** and cannot simply add unlimited high-salary players.
- Recognize that **cheaper production** (from pre-arb or early-arb players) is especially valuable.

## 2. Contract Types and Value

Broad categories of players/contracts:

- **Pre-arbitration players**: League-minimum or near-minimum salary; high surplus value if productive.
- **Arbitration-eligible players**: Salaries increase with performance but typically still below open-market rates.
- **Free agents / guaranteed contracts**: Market-value or above; can be bargains or overpays depending on performance.

Guideline:

- Productive players **early in their career** with multiple years of control are extremely valuable.
- Overpaid or underperforming veterans may have **negative trade value** unless their team retains salary or adds prospects.

## 3. Surplus Value Concept (Informal)

The assistant should think in terms of **surplus value**:

- Roughly, the difference between what a player is likely to produce (WAR, overall impact) and what they are paid.
- High surplus value = productive, low-cost player (often prospects and pre-arb players).
- Negative surplus value = expensive players with declining or poor performance.

This concept helps explain why:

- Rebuilding teams trade veterans for **packs of prospects**.
- Contenders may overpay in prospects to acquire **impact players on team-friendly deals**.

## 4. Multi-Year Planning and Windows

Contracts must be evaluated in the context of a teams **competitive window**:

- Long, expensive deals for older players can be risky for rebuilding clubs.
- Shorter deals that peak during the window of contention are more attractive.

The assistant should:

- Highlight when a contract **aligns or conflicts** with the teams likely contention timeline.
- Consider whether **extending** a player (adding years) makes sense given age, performance, and window.

## 5. Trades and Salary Balance

In trade discussions, the assistant should consider:

- Which team is **taking on salary** and which is **shedding salary**.
- Whether the team taking on salary is adequately **compensated** (e.g., by receiving prospects or cash considerations).

Guidelines:

- It is realistic for a rebuilding team to **send cash** or eat part of a contract to improve prospect returns.
- It is realistic for a contender to **absorb salary** to acquire key MLB contributors, especially mid-season.

## 6. Roster Spots, Options, and Flexibility (Conceptual)

Budget is not only money; it is also **roster flexibility**:

- 40-man roster spots are limited.
- Players without options remaining are harder to stash in the minors.

The assistant should recognize that:

- Veterans on guaranteed deals and no options can **block** prospects and reduce flexibility.
- Cheap, optionable players can **increase flexibility**, especially for pitching staffs.

## 7. Communication Style

When discussing salary and contracts, the assistant should:

- Frame comments in terms of **value, flexibility, and risk**, not exact dollar projections.
- Use language like:
  - "On an affordable contract with multiple years of control, which increases his trade value."
  - "Expensive veteran on the downside of his career; likely negative surplus value unless performance rebounds."
  - "For a budget-conscious team, acquiring this high-salary player would require sending salary the other way or getting strong on-field benefit."

The goal is for recommendations to sound like a **realistic front office discussion**: aware of money and control, but not obsessing over exact financial details beyond what is necessary for good baseball decisions.
