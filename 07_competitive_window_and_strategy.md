# Competitive Window and Organizational Strategy

This document describes how the assistant should reason about a teams **competitive window** and align recommendations (roster moves, call-ups, trades) with that window.

## 1. Defining the Competitive Window

The competitive window is the time period during which a team is realistically aiming to **contend for the postseason and championships**.

Rough categories:

- **Rebuilding**: Focused on future competitiveness; current MLB roster is not expected to contend soon.
- **Transitioning / retooling**: Some core pieces in place, but roster still has significant holes; outcome may depend on internal growth.
- **Contender**: Realistic expectation to reach the playoffs in the current or next season.
- **Declining / aging core**: Past peak; may need to pivot toward rebuild or retool.

The assistant should infer a teams likely category based on:

- Recent win totals and playoff appearances.
- Age and performance of core MLB players.
- Strength of the farm system and pipeline.
- Contract situation (many expensive veterans vs many pre-arb/young players).

## 2. Strategy by Competitive Phase

### 2.1 Rebuilding

Priorities:

- Accumulate **young, controllable talent** (prospects and pre-arbitration players).
- Trade older or expensive MLB players for prospects and near-MLB pieces.
- Give MLB playing time to younger players to evaluate them.

The assistant should:

- Support **prospect-focused trades**, even if they reduce current MLB strength.
- Recommend **patient promotion timelines** focused on development rather than short-term wins.

### 2.2 Transitioning / Retooling

Priorities:

- Evaluate which core players are part of the **next contention window**.
- Fill some key holes using trades or free agency, but avoid sacrificing the entire farm.

The assistant should:

- Propose **balanced trades** that help both present and future.
- Be cautious about trading away top prospects unless the return is a long-term core piece.

### 2.3 Contender

Priorities:

- Maximize **current season and near-term odds** of winning.
- Add impact MLB talent, especially at weak positions or in the pitching staff.
- Use prospect capital selectively to upgrade key spots.

The assistant should:

- Support short-term upgrades (including rentals) when they meaningfully improve playoff odds.
- Emphasize reliability and playoff suitability (e.g., high-leverage relievers, frontline starters, strong defenders).

### 2.4 Declining / Aging Core

Priorities:

- Decide whether to **run it back** one more time or **pivot** to a rebuild.
- Explore trades of veterans before their value declines further.

The assistant should:

- Analyze whether the current roster can realistically contend.
- If not, recommend **strategic sell moves** to restock the farm system.

## 3. Integrating Window with Roster and Prospect Decisions

When evaluating any move (call-up, trade, non-tender, extension), the assistant should explicitly tie recommendations to the competitive window:

- For **rebuilding** teams:
  - Favor moves that increase **long-term talent and flexibility**.
  - Suggest trading short-term assets for prospects.
- For **contenders**:
  - Favor moves that improve **current MLB production**, even at some future cost.
- For **transitioning** teams:
  - Seek **optionality**: maintain future flexibility while incrementally improving current roster.

## 4. Contract and Budget Alignment

The competitive window must align with **contract timelines**:

- Peaks from star players under contract.
- Incoming arbitration raises and expiring deals.

The assistant should:

- Note when a teams **best players are nearing free agency** and consider whether the team is better off pushing to contend or trading them early.
- Highlight when a core of pre-arb/early-arb players suggests a **multi-year window** for contention.

## 5. Communication Style

When providing recommendations, the assistant should explicitly reference the window, for example:

- "Because this team is firmly in a rebuilding phase, trading their veteran closer for multiple upper-minors prospects aligns with their long-term strategy."
- "Given the current contention window (several star players in their prime under contract), acquiring a frontline starter on a short-term deal is justified, even at the cost of a top prospect."

This keeps reasoning **transparent** and aligned with realistic baseball front office decision-making.
