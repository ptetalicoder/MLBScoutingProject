## 1. Example: Calling Up a Minor League Hitter

**Scenario:** The organization is considering calling up OF Jake Ramirez (AAA) to the MLB roster.

### 1. Concise summary (1–2 sentences)

Jake Ramirez looks close to MLB-ready as a left-handed corner outfield bat, but an immediate promotion should be framed as a **platoon or bench role**, not as an everyday solution. Another few weeks in AAA would carry low risk and allow further consolidation of recent gains.

### 2. Context paragraph

Ramirez is a 25-year-old left-handed hitter currently in AAA, posting strong overall numbers over the past season and a half. The MLB club is contending and has received below-average production from the left field position, particularly versus right-handed pitching. The team has adequate short-term depth, but lacks a long-term, cost-controlled option in the corner outfield spots.

### 3. Analysis paragraph

Over his last ~700 AAA plate appearances, Ramirez has produced an OPS around .850 with a wRC+ in the 125–130 range, supported by an OBP in the mid-.360s and an ISO near .200. His K% sits in a manageable low-20s range with a healthy walk rate around 11–12%, and batted-ball data shows a solid hard-hit rate with most damage against right-handed pitching. Defensively, he grades as adequate in a corner with average speed and a playable arm, making him a bat-driven profile but not a liability in the field.

### 4. Options with benefits and risks

- **Option A – Call up Ramirez now as a platoon/bench bat**
  - **Benefits:**
    - Immediately upgrades MLB production versus right-handed pitching at a position of need.
    - Begins his MLB adjustment period while the team is contending, potentially adding low-cost production.
    - Provides a left-handed bat off the bench with on-base skill and power.
  - **Risks:**
    - If he struggles early, confidence and perceived value could dip.
    - Limited MLB track record; some of his AAA success may not fully translate.

- **Option B – Keep Ramirez in AAA for a bit longer**
  - **Benefits:**
    - Allows continued refinement of plate discipline and defensive reps with everyday playing time.
    - Reduces pressure of immediate MLB performance in the middle of a pennant race.
    - Provides further data to confirm that recent gains are stable, not a short hot streak.
  - **Risks:**
    - Delays potentially useful MLB production at a position of weakness.
    - Slight risk of injury or regression while still in AAA.

- **Option C – Explore including Ramirez in a trade package**
  - **Benefits:**
    - Could help acquire a more established MLB outfielder or pitcher for the current contention window.
    - Uses Ramirez’s strong upper-minors stats to enhance prospect value.
  - **Risks:**
    - If Ramirez becomes a solid MLB regular elsewhere, trade may be viewed as selling low on cost-controlled years.
    - Reduces internal depth and long-term flexibility in the outfield.

### 5. Final, honest recommendation

**Recommended course of action:** Keep Ramirez in AAA for a short additional period while monitoring performance and MLB roster health, with a clear plan to promote him as a **platoon/bench bat** against right-handed pitching in the near term. This balances his strong statistical case for a promotion with the benefit of slightly more seasoning and maintains optionality for trade discussions.

## 2. Example: Evaluating a Trade Proposal

**Scenario:** Contending Team A considers trading two top-100 prospects for SP Alex Turner (established MLB starter with two years of control) from Rebuilding Team B.

### 1. Concise summary

Trading two top-100 prospects for Alex Turner is an aggressive but defensible move for a clear contender needing a mid-rotation upgrade. However, it meaningfully reduces long-term talent depth and should only be pursued if Turner projects as a clear playoff-rotation piece.

### 2. Context paragraph

Team A is firmly in its contention window with multiple star position players under contract for the next 2–3 seasons, but the rotation behind its ace is thin and has battled injuries. Team B is in a rebuilding phase with limited short-term playoff hopes and a strong incentive to convert veterans into prospects. Turner is a durable right-handed starter with a solid track record and two more years of team control at a market-reasonable salary.

### 3. Analysis paragraph

Over the past three seasons, Turner has averaged ~3 WAR per year with an ERA and FIP both in the low-3s, supported by a K% in the mid-20s and a walk rate below 7%. He consistently provides 180+ innings when healthy and handles both right- and left-handed hitters without extreme splits. The two prospects in question project as one potential everyday position player and one mid-rotation starter, but both are at AA with inherent development risk. For Team A, the question is whether two prime years of Turner at a high but not elite level outweigh the potential long-term value and low cost of the prospects.

### 4. Options with benefits and risks

- **Option A – Make the proposed trade (2 top-100 prospects for Turner)**
  - **Benefits:**
    - Immediately stabilizes Team A’s rotation with a 2/3-type starter for two seasons.
    - Strongly increases playoff odds and improves the projected playoff rotation.
    - Aligns with Team A’s current contention window while stars are under contract.
  - **Risks:**
    - Gives up significant long-term value and cheap production potential.
    - If Turner is injured or declines, the trade could become a net negative quickly.

- **Option B – Attempt to negotiate a smaller prospect cost**
  - **Benefits:**
    - May preserve one high-upside prospect while still acquiring Turner.
    - Maintains more long-term flexibility and depth in the farm system.
  - **Risks:**
    - Team B may reject a lighter offer or seek other bidders.
    - Prolonged negotiations risk losing the opportunity entirely.

- **Option C – Keep the prospects and pursue a lower-tier or rental starter**
  - **Benefits:**
    - Retains long-term upside and surplus value from the prospects.
    - Reduces risk of paying a premium in talent for two years of Turner.
  - **Risks:**
    - Might not materially improve playoff odds if the acquired starter is only a marginal upgrade.
    - Could waste part of the current contention window if pitching remains a clear weakness.

### 5. Final, honest recommendation

**Recommended course of action:** Explore acquiring Turner but initially push for a slightly lighter package (e.g., one top-100 prospect plus secondary pieces). If Team A’s internal projections view its current window as especially valuable and Turner as a strong two-year playoff rotation piece, accepting the original 2-prospect cost can be justified. However, if there are reasonable alternatives on the market, holding onto both top-100 prospects is a defensible choice.

## 3. Example: Individual Player Evaluation (Hitter)

**Player:** 3B/1B Chris Dalton, age 27, MLB.

### 1. Concise overview

Chris Dalton currently profiles as a bat-first corner infielder with above-average power and on-base skills but below-average defense. He can be an everyday regular on a contender if supported by adequate defensive alignments, or a strong-side platoon/1B-DH option otherwise.

### 2. Context paragraph

Dalton has spent the last two seasons in the majors, primarily at third base and first base, for a mid-tier team. Offensively, he has delivered back-to-back seasons of above-average production, while defensive metrics at third base are mixed to negative. The team has emerging prospects at the infield corners and is deciding whether to build around Dalton, trade him, or shift his role.

### 3. Analysis paragraph

Offensively, Dalton has posted a wRC+ around 120 over the last two years with a .250–.260 AVG, OBP in the .340–.350 range, and SLG near .470–.480, supported by an ISO in the .200 range. His K% sits in the low-20s with a solid walk rate near 10%, and his hard-hit% is comfortably above league average. Defensively, metrics suggest he is below-average at third base but playable at first base, making him more valuable at 1B/DH or as a part-time 3B. He is in his late prime years with several seasons of team control remaining.

### 4. Positives and negatives

- **Positives:**
  - Consistent above-average offensive production over multiple MLB seasons.
  - Strong plate discipline and power profile with solid K/BB balance.
  - Flexible enough to cover both 3B and 1B, adding some roster flexibility.
  - Prime-age player with remaining team control.

- **Negatives / Risks:**
  - Below-average defense at 3B limits optimal deployment and may worsen with age.
  - Bat is good but not truly elite for a full-time DH or 1B-only role.
  - Limited baserunning impact; value is very bat-centric.

### 5. Final consensus evaluation

Dalton projects as an above-average regular at 1B/3B/DH on a competitive team, best utilized with at least some time away from third base to protect his defensive limitations. His offensive track record supports building him into the team’s medium-term plans, though he is more of a strong supporting piece than a franchise cornerstone.

## 4. Example: Individual Player Evaluation (Pitcher)

**Player:** RHP Marcus Lee, age 24, AAA.

### 1. Concise overview

Marcus Lee profiles as a near-MLB-ready right-handed starter with mid-rotation upside but also a realistic path as a high-leverage reliever. His strikeout and walk profile at AAA suggests that he could handle MLB innings soon, though command consistency remains the key variable.

### 2. Context paragraph

Lee has spent the full season in AAA as a starter, logging a substantial workload for a playoff-contending organization with thin rotation depth behind its top two starters. He is age-appropriate for the level, and this is his second year facing upper-minors competition. The organization must decide whether to promote him to cover MLB innings, continue refining him as a starter in AAA, or shift him to a shorter relief role.

### 3. Analysis paragraph

In AAA this season, Lee has posted an ERA in the mid-3s with an FIP slightly better, driven by a K% in the high-20s and a BB% around 9%. His WHIP is solidly better than league average, and he has limited home runs reasonably well, suggesting a combination of decent contact management and strikeout ability. Start-by-start logs show occasional command lapses leading to short outings, but overall durability is encouraging. The pitch mix (as described in scouting) supports a starter look, but the statistical profile leaves open a high-impact bullpen path if command stalls.

### 4. Positives and negatives

- **Positives:**
  - Strong strikeout rates at AAA with solid run prevention.
  - Age-appropriate performance at an upper level with a starter’s workload.
  - Multiple potential paths to MLB value (mid-rotation starter or late-inning reliever).

- **Negatives / Risks:**
  - Walk rate is borderline for a full-time MLB starter and may be punished more at the next level.
  - Occasional short outings suggest volatility and incomplete command refinement.
  - Transitioning role mid-season (starter → reliever) carries some uncertainty and adjustment cost.

### 5. Final consensus evaluation

Lee projects most realistically as a mid-rotation starter if command stabilizes, with a strong fallback as a high-leverage reliever. In a contending context, a cautious promotion as a multi-inning or lower-leverage starter, with the option to transition to the bullpen, would be a reasonable path once organizational depth and innings limits are considered.
